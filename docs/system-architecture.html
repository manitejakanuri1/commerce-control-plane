System Architecture

The Commerce Control Plane treats a sale as a strict pipeline of separable responsibilities: the model may only propose, and every rupee is decided by code.

The shopper and content agents ask the gate directly. The closer asks through her history — what she viewed, owns and previously refused is what the gate prices against. Growth asks nothing at all: it proposes no price, so it only reads what the gate already decided.

SHOPPER · built

Reads names, prices and stock. Never a cost. Any product code it invents is dropped before the engine sees it.

GROWTH · built

The only one that does not ask. It reads the audit — every decision the gate already made — and tells the owner which of them is costing them money.

CONTENT · next

Everyone can write ad copy. Nobody else can ask whether the product is in stock and whether its margin is worth advertising.

CLOSER · next

Reads her history first, then lets the gate price against it. Only once it approves does the voice agent dial, and a payment link exists only after she has said yes.

THE LOOP

The gate decides. The audit records it. Growth reads the audit and finds the pattern. Content rewrites what was losing money — and asks the gate. Nothing recommends a price without the gate having approved it first, and nothing recommends at all without the gate having been asked before.

Two diamonds

One branches on money — the cap allowed it, does the margin? One branches on truth — silence is not failure, so what actually happened?

Refused is not a dead end

A refusal flows into the audit alongside a confirmed sale. A merchant asking why something didn't sell gets an answer.

The engine is elsewhere

It runs on the merchant's own server. Cost never crosses the network, and a database grant enforces that rather than a promise.

The growth agent, in detail

The only agent that reads what a shop spends. Ad platforms count sales; this counts profit, because it is the only thing in the loop that knows what each product earns.

Why the join matters

Meta reports a cable ad as the winner: forty sales, cheapest cost per sale. It does not know a cable earns Rs 120 and cost Rs 250 to sell. The join is the whole product.

Ranked by rupees

Six true observations are not a plan. An owner with one spare hour needs to know which one to spend it on, so findings are ordered by what they are worth, not by what is easiest to say.

The honesty gate

Below twenty events it reports that rather than advising. Advice made from four data points reads exactly like advice made from four thousand, and an owner acts on it either way.

The loop before the gate

Editor and writer iterate against a rubric until the voice passes. That part is ordinary, and it is the part every marketing tool already has.

The gate is the product

A content agent that does not know your stock will write a beautiful ad for something you sold out of last week, and spend real money running it.

Blocks are audited too

A refused ad is recorded next to a published one, so a merchant can see what was almost spent and why it was not.

The closer, in detail

Two questions before anyone is contacted. Is the discount profitable — and is this cart worth the cost of reaching out at all? A three-rupee call on a two-hundred-rupee cart loses money. Only once the gate has approved does the voice agent dial, and only once she says yes does a payment link exist.

The second question is the moat

Every cart-recovery tool asks whether the discount is allowed. None of them can ask whether the customer is worth a phone call, because none of them knows the margin.

History decides the offer

Two shoppers abandon the same cart and are quoted differently, because one has bought twice before and one is new. The audit is not a record here — it is the input.

The link is minted last

Created the moment she says yes, and it lands on her phone while she is still on the line. A link sent before that is a discount handed to somebody who never asked for one — and who might have paid full price.

Why every offer is logged

Two shoppers paid different prices for the same saree. One of them may eventually ask why, and without the row there is no answer — not a vague one, none at all.
